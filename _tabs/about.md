# ElseRoot | ಪ್ರಸ್ಥಾನ

## Unfamiliar Ideas. Serious Inquiry.

**ElseRoot gives unfamiliar ideas the room to develop, the scrutiny to become stronger, and the freedom to challenge established assumptions.**

We publish essays and frameworks that offer more than criticism. They must construct, explain, or propose something worth examining.

## Why ElseRoot | ಪ್ರಸ್ಥಾನ?

Many valuable ideas do not fit comfortably within established publications, familiar disciplines, or accepted narratives.

Some begin with unusual questions. Others connect fields that are usually kept apart. Some revisit foundational assumptions that have become invisible through familiarity.

An unfamiliar idea is not valuable merely because it is unfamiliar. It must be explained clearly, tested against serious objections, and connected to evidence, experience, or reason.

ElseRoot exists to provide that space.

**ElseRoot** represents an alternative intellectual foundation. It is a place where an idea can begin from a different premise, develop its reasoning, encounter serious criticism, and establish grounds of its own.

**Prasthāna | ಪ್ರಸ್ಥಾನ** is its Indic companion. The term can signify a setting forth, a point of departure, a path, a standpoint, or an established intellectual position.

The two names express complementary aspects of the publication:

- **ElseRoot** represents the different foundation from which an unfamiliar idea may grow.
- **Prasthāna | ಪ್ರಸ್ಥಾನ** represents the point from which that idea sets forth on its intellectual journey.

Together, they describe a publication that gives unfamiliar ideas both a foundation and a direction.

## What We Publish

ElseRoot organizes its work across four broad domains. These domains are distinguished not merely by subject, but by the central question an essay asks.

### Artha | Resources

**ಅರ್ಥ**

Artha concerns resources, value, livelihood, enterprise, and the practical organization of material and professional life.

This domain includes essays on professional life, careers, management, organizations, enterprise, markets, economics, and the creation and stewardship of value.

Artha asks:

> How do individuals and institutions create, organize, use, and sustain resources and value?

### Tantra | Technology

**ತಂತ್ರ**

Tantra concerns systems, methods, instruments, techniques, and the purposeful application of knowledge.

This domain includes essays on engineering, technology, software, architecture, computation, artificial intelligence, systems thinking, tools, methods, and designed processes.

Tantra asks:

> How do systems work, and how can they be constructed, operated, or transformed?

### Dharma | Duties

**ಧರ್ಮ**

Dharma concerns responsibility, conduct, institutions, relationships, social order, and the principles that sustain individual and collective life.

This domain includes essays on ethics, leadership, governance, justice, institutions, culture, civic life, accountability, professional conduct, and social responsibility.

Dharma asks:

> What should guide individuals and institutions, and what enables society to endure?

**Nīti** is treated as a practical dimension of Dharma, not as a separate publication domain. It includes leadership judgment, policy, statecraft, institutional strategy, professional conduct, and decision-making under real-world constraints.

### Jñāna | Inquiry

**ಜ್ಞಾನ**

Jñāna concerns knowledge, understanding, investigation, interpretation, and the means by which claims about reality are established.

This domain includes essays on science, mathematics, philosophy, history, language, civilization, traditional knowledge, research, and the foundations of human understanding.

Jñāna asks:

> What is true, what does it mean, and how can we know it?

## Where Domains Meet

The domains are distinct, but they are not isolated.

An essay about artificial intelligence may concern:

- technical architecture under Tantra;
- business consequences under Artha;
- social responsibilities under Dharma;
- or underlying theory under Jñāna.

An essay about economics may examine:

- markets and value under Artha;
- economic modelling under Tantra;
- economic justice under Dharma;
- or the meaning and measurement of value under Jñāna.

The primary domain is determined by the central question of the article. Secondary subjects and perspectives are represented using tags.

## Editorial Principles

ElseRoot values:

- independent inquiry without manufactured contrarianism;
- practical experience supported by careful reasoning;
- first-principles examination of accepted assumptions;
- clear distinctions between facts, interpretations, hypotheses, proposals, and predictions;
- criticism accompanied by constructive alternatives;
- serious engagement with counterarguments;
- accessible writing without intellectual oversimplification;
- Indian-rooted perspectives that engage confidently with the modern world;
- and freedom to develop ideas across conventional disciplinary boundaries.

ElseRoot does not require contributors to agree with its existing essays.

Contributors must make their assumptions visible, support factual claims, address credible objections, and treat readers as capable of forming their own judgments.

## Contributing to ElseRoot

ElseRoot is currently being developed as an independent publication. Formal submissions are not yet open.

In the future, ElseRoot may invite carefully developed contributions in English and Kannada.

A contribution should do more than provide commentary. A strong contribution should accomplish at least one of the following:

- define an important problem more precisely;
- question a foundational assumption;
- construct a useful framework;
- connect fields usually examined separately;
- document a meaningful practical experience;
- recover and carefully interpret a neglected source;
- explain an unfamiliar idea clearly;
- or propose an alternative that can be examined, challenged, or tested.

Unfamiliarity alone is not enough.

A serious contribution must distinguish among:

- documented facts;
- personal observations;
- interpretations;
- analogies;
- hypotheses;
- proposals;
- and predictions.

Contributors should engage credible objections and identify the limits of their arguments.

### Languages

ElseRoot intends to support writing in:

- English
- ಕನ್ನಡ | Kannada

Bilingual articles are welcome when both versions preserve the meaning and intellectual character of the work rather than providing a mechanical translation.
